## Situation read

Düsseldorf-Holthausen is Henkel's headquarters and one of its two biggest production sites. It makes laundry detergents, household cleaners, adhesives for consumers and tradespeople, and products for industrial customers [R20]. The site has its own combined heat and power plant with five natural-gas units and 75 MW electric capacity. Four are back-pressure steam turbines and one is a gas turbine, so the electricity comes as a by-product of the steam. The plant has a medium-voltage grid connection with partial feed-in [R42, R46, R48]. The plant also supplies other companies on the site and the grid. Henkel books 168 856 of its 357 850 t Scope 1 emissions as supplied to third parties [R15, R49]. In the last three years coal use stopped (2024) and the coal-firing infrastructure was dismantled, two small units were shut down in July 2025, capacity went from 89 to 75 MW, and since April 2026 the plant sells waste heat to Stadtwerke Düsseldorf for district heating [R10, R47, R37, R8]. In 2025 the plant emitted 211 078 tCO2, after 229 042 in 2024, all of it under the EU ETS [R50, R51]. Free allocation was 61 633 allowances, so about 71 % had to be bought, roughly €11 million at the 2025 auction price for the whole plant [R52; inferred from R50, R52, R36]. The group has already beaten its 2030 target, so the driver for the plant is the net-zero path. Henkel calls the plant a material asset with locked-in emissions and plans efficiency gains, more biomethane and waste-heat use by 2030. Gas costs around 4 cents per kWh and purchased electricity around 13 cents [R53, R9, R10; inferred from R31, R28]. Not public: how much of the plant's steam, power and CO2 is Henkel's own, and how many tons the site makes. Both are site-visit items. The first one is the fact that would change this read the most [R15, R19, R50].
